Pumpwise Privacy Policy

Last updated: 13 November 2026

Pumpwise shows official fuel prices near you. It is built to need as little of your information as possible. This policy explains what the app does and does not do with data, who is responsible, and what your rights are.

Who is responsible

Pumpwise is made and published by an independent developer in Australia ("Pumpwise", "we"). For anything in this policy, write to tyjcraw@gmail.com. Under European data protection law Pumpwise is the "controller" for the little processing described here.

The short version

  • Pumpwise has no accounts, no sign-in and no analytics. A small banner advert keeps the app free. The advert and the app's check for updates are the only times a third party receives information about your device.
  • Your location is used on your iPhone to work out distances. It is never sent to us or stored by us.
  • Your settings (fuel, search area, fill-up size, fuel economy, recent places) stay on your iPhone.
  • The fuel price website has no cookies and no tracking, and its "Show stations near me" button uses your location only inside your browser.
  • We keep no personal data about you, so there is nothing for us to sell, share or lose.

Your location

With your permission, Pumpwise reads your location while the app is open. It uses it on your device to work out which stations are near you and how far away they are, and to show the name of your area (looked up by Apple's own map service on your iPhone). You give that permission in the iPhone prompt and can withdraw it at any time in the iPhone Settings app; the app then works by searching around a town or postcode instead.

To get prices, the app downloads public price files from the Pumpwise feed on GitHub Pages. Each file covers an area of roughly 50 by 50 kilometres, so the request shows only which broad area you are in, in the same way any web request reveals an approximate location from your internet address. GitHub may keep routine server logs as described in the GitHub Privacy Statement at https://docs.github.com/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement. Pumpwise does not receive, keep or see these logs. These downloads are necessary to provide the service you asked for.

Pumpwise never collects location in the background and never stores your location history.

The fuel price website

The fuel price pages at https://tjcraw6589.github.io/pumpwise/prices/ have no accounts, no cookies, no adverts, no analytics and no tracking.

If you tap "Show stations near me", your browser first asks whether the page may use your location. If you allow it, the location is used only inside your browser to work out which stations are closest and how far away they are. The page then downloads the same public price files the app uses, each covering roughly 50 by 50 kilometres, so the request shows only which broad area you are in. Your location is never sent to us or stored, and the page forgets it when you leave. You can say no and search for a town instead.

The search box downloads one public list of place names and searches it inside your browser; what you type is not sent anywhere. The language and the fuel you choose are remembered in your browser's own storage on your device and are never sent to us. Directions links open Google Maps, which is covered by Google's own privacy policy.

Place search

If you search for a place, the words you type are sent to Apple's map service to find it, as when you search in Apple Maps. UK postcodes are looked up with postcodes.io, a free public service built on Ordnance Survey and Office for National Statistics open data (https://postcodes.io/about). Nothing else is sent with a search.

Directions

When you tap Directions, Pumpwise opens Apple Maps, Google Maps or Waze with the station's position. What happens next is covered by that app's own privacy policy.

Data you choose

Your fuel, search area, fill-up size, fuel economy, members-only choice, appearance and recent places are saved in the app's private storage on your iPhone. They are never transmitted. Deleting the app removes them.

Advertising

Pumpwise shows a small banner advert using the Google Mobile Ads SDK (AdMob). To serve and measure adverts, Google may collect information such as your device's advertising identifier, IP address, approximate location derived from it, device model and operating system, and interactions with adverts. How Google uses this information is described in Google's Privacy Policy at https://policies.google.com/privacy and at https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

  • On iOS, the App Tracking Transparency prompt lets you allow or refuse tracking. If you refuse, adverts are still shown but are not personalised using your identifier.
  • In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Google's consent form is shown before personalised adverts are requested, and Settings offers "Ad privacy choices" to change your answer at any time. Personalised advertising there relies on your consent; without it you see non-personalised adverts.
  • You can reset or limit your advertising identifier in your iPhone settings at any time.

Your precise location is never shared with the advertising SDK.

App updates

So that small fixes can reach you without a new App Store version, Pumpwise checks Expo's update service (EAS Update, run by Expo) when it opens and downloads any update for your version of the app. The check sends your iPhone's platform, the app version and update channel, and a random installation number that the service uses to count how many phones it serves. It does not include your location, your settings or anything you type. Like any web service, Expo also sees your internet address. This check is in our legitimate interest in keeping the app working and secure. Expo's privacy policy is at https://expo.dev/privacy.

Where information goes

Google, Expo and GitHub are based in the United States, so the technical information described above may be processed outside your country. Each of them states that it relies on recognised safeguards for transfers from the European Union and the United Kingdom, such as the EU-US Data Privacy Framework or standard contractual clauses; see their privacy policies linked above. We ourselves transfer nothing, because we receive nothing.

How long

We keep nothing. Your settings stay on your phone until you delete the app. Google and Expo keep the technical information they collect for the periods stated in their own policies.

Your rights

If you are in the European Union, the United Kingdom, Switzerland or another place with similar rules, you have the right to ask for access to your personal data, to have it corrected or deleted, to restrict or object to its processing, to receive it in a portable form, and to withdraw consent at any time without affecting what was done before. Because we hold no personal data about you, most of these requests will have the same answer: there is nothing to find. Requests about advertising data go to Google; you can also change your consent in Settings under "Ad privacy choices" and in your iPhone's privacy settings. Write to tyjcraw@gmail.com and we will reply within a month.

You also have the right to complain to a data protection authority: in Spain the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos (www.aepd.es), in France the Commission nationale de l'informatique et des libertés (www.cnil.fr), in Portugal the Comissão Nacional de Proteção de Dados (www.cnpd.pt), in the United Kingdom the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk), in Australia the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (www.oaic.gov.au), or your own country's authority.
